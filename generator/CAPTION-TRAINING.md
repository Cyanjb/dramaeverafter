# Caption training corpus

Cyan, 24 Aug 2026: "I need you to get to the point where you can write these
captions on your own so consider corrections and my updates to text as
training."

This file is that training set. Every pair below is verbatim: DRAFT is what a
session wrote, CYAN is what she changed it to (with only mechanical typo fixes
applied). The lesson classes are ordered by how often she had to make the
correction. **Read this file before writing any batch. After every review,
diff her edits against the drafts, add pairs that teach something new, and
update the metrics table.** Git history holds every pair ever; this file holds
the ones that teach.

## Class 1 — the ending is one beat, and it must land

She added a landing where a caption trailed off, and she CUT second beats
where a caption landed twice. Both directions, same rule.

Added:
- DRAFT: "…neither of them has told the truth about themselves yet."
  CYAN: "…And still neither of them has told the truth about themselves yet.
  Wait until they find out."
- DRAFT: "…She spends the whole marriage looking down on him."
  CYAN: "…Little does she know that without Eli her life will be much harder
  and time is running out."
- DRAFT: "…Dawn's response to the whole situation is to run, and what she is
  running from is the interesting part."
  CYAN: "…Dawn's response to the whole situation is to run. But why?"

Cut (the sharper beat was already there):
- DRAFT: "…the people who pulled them apart in the first place have not gone
  anywhere. Now there is a child to protect as well."
  CYAN: "…the people who pulled them apart in the first place have not given
  up."
- DRAFT: "…Avenge her father, or forgive the man she loves. Which would you
  choose?"
  CYAN: ends at "…forgive the man she loves."
- DRAFT: "…she wants everything back. Watching them beg on their knees is
  deeply satisfying."
  CYAN: ends at "…she wants everything back."

The cut pattern is precise: rhetorical questions about the STORY survive
("But why?", "Is this fate?", "Will they ever break their curses?"); direct
challenges to the reader ("Which would you choose?") and commentary about the
viewing experience ("deeply satisfying", "Say it isn't so") get cut. Tease the
story, never narrate the audience.

## Class 2 — plain speech beats constructed cleverness

- DRAFT hook: "Chemistry lessons, paid for in fake boyfriend hours."
  CYAN: "Chemistry lessons in exchange for a fake boyfriend"
- DRAFT hook: "The title is a promise, and this one keeps it."
  CYAN: "She's going to make him pay."
- DRAFT hook: "The baby is not her husband's. That is the good news."
  CYAN: "The baby isn't her husband's."
- DRAFT: "with the wedding almost on top of her"
  CYAN: "with the wedding just around the corner"
- DRAFT hook: "Her husband murdered her. Then she woke up earlier."
  CYAN: "Her husband murdered her. Then she woke up."

The last one is the whole class in five words: the extra word that "adds
information" usually subtracts punch.

## Class 3 — syntax knots from dodging the source

Rotating a sentence away from its source produces broken English. Her fixes
are always plain subject-verb order. (Root cause and the measurable shadow
test live in the skill; these are the reference pairs.)

- DRAFT: "It is not, because of who he turns out to be to the man she nearly
  married"
  CYAN: "That is not how things play out though because his identity is not
  as simple as it seems"
- DRAFT: "the woman's grandson is how she meets Austin"
  CYAN: "she meets the woman's grandson Austin"
- DRAFT: "Declan married her only to honour his grandfather's dying wish, and
  he is cruel to her with it."
  CYAN: "Declan married her only to honour his grandfather's dying wish,
  nothing more. So she is left to endure Declan's cruelty."

## Class 4 — never invent the HOW when the source gives the THAT

Her words: "they don't just talk you invented that".

- DRAFT: "The doctor who answers spends the night trying to talk her back
  from the edge of that decision" (source: "determined to pull her back")
  CYAN: "The doctor who answers determinedly tries to change her very
  dangerous decision."
- DRAFT: "she helps an older woman who has been scammed, and the woman's
  grandson comes to thank her" (the thanks was invented)
  CYAN: "…and she meets the woman's grandson Austin."

Same family, self-caught on readback: "a mafia king with bodies behind him"
(source said "murderous"), "no other way to keep her mother alive" (source
said medical bills), "she does it well" (source said "tries her best").

## Class 5 — don't drop the source's biggest beat

Her words: "you left out the most important part where she holds them
accountable".

- DRAFT: grief → foundation → he exiles himself (revenge beat missing)
  CYAN adds: "She makes both Candace and Jace pay for what they did."
- DRAFT: ended at "he pulls away." (source's climax dropped for length)
  CYAN adds: "Will facing life and death bring them closer together or
  separate them for good?"

Corollary: the spoiler rule protects the TURN, not the premise. Where the
title itself announces the reveal, she puts it in:
- DRAFT: "…every time he moves against her a janitor happens to be standing
  in the way." CYAN adds: "A janitor who turns out to be the richest man in
  the world."

## Class 6 — the audience's words, kept

- DRAFT: "Cassandra marries fast" → CYAN: "Cassandra flash marries a man…"
  and her hook: "A husband, two stepkids, and a flash marriage."
- DRAFT: "chief executive" → CYAN ruling: "you removed CEO … should have been
  left in."
- Her own wording elsewhere: "They agree to have a contract marriage."

But it is vocabulary, not incantation: she herself wrote "a fling" where the
source said "one night stand", and "ran the biggest defense contractor" where
it said CEO. Keep the terms the audience browses by; do not bolt them on.

## Class 7 — contractions and flow are the register

- DRAFT: "it is the night before the final again. This time she is not
  wasting her second chance"
  CYAN: "it's the night before the final again. This time she's not wasting
  her second chance … No. This time she'll simply watch them destroy
  themselves"
- DRAFT: "He gets her out. What sits between them is…"
  CYAN: "After he gets her out what sits between them is…"

Full forms survive where a line wants weight ("She does not come back
gently" lived for a week; her replacement "She doesn't come back the same"
swapped the word, kept the weight). Heavy material stays uncontracted.

## The loop (every batch from now on)

1. Read this file and ten live captions before writing.
2. Write. Self-check against every class above, then check + readback +
   shadow audit as usual.
3. After her review: diff every edit, classify against these classes. A pair
   that fits an existing class means the training has not landed — say so.
   A pair that teaches something NEW gets added here with its lesson.
4. Update the metrics table. The number she cares about is the edit rate
   heading toward "I only spot-check these".
